This site is not a blog; it's a digital garden. The difference comes down to one thing: a blog's unit is *the finished article*, a garden's unit is *the growing note*. Every post here wears a maturity badge — 🌱 seed for a rough, freshly planted note, 🌿 growing for an understanding that's taking shape, 🌲 evergreen for something stable enough to cite. Seeds never have to apologize for being rough.

<Callout emoji="🌱">
  If you open a seed and think "this isn't finished" — correct, it isn't. Publishing early is
  part of the rules, not a failure of them.
</Callout>

## Why it works this way

I have ADHD. For me, the task "write a complete blog post" has an activation cost so high it almost never happens — but "prune today's paper-reading notes and put them out" is doable. So the core rule of this garden is: **I never write from scratch**. Every post is a byproduct of study — paper deep-dives, course notes, experiment logs — planted in its roughest viable form, then pruned a little on each revisit.

Maintenance follows the same principle: publishing is compressed into a single `git push`, and everything after that (build, deploy, going live) happens automatically. I tend this pipeline together with my agent (Claude) — it turns notes into drafts, checks facts, and handles the release chores, while I do the actual understanding and judgment. Which makes this site a small living experiment for the Loops track, too.

## The three tracks

- **Latents** — generative models: diffusion, image & video generation, conditioning, efficiency.
- **Logs** — systems: MIT 6.824 distributed systems, Designing Machine Learning Systems.
- **Loops** — agentic systems: tool use, orchestration, evaluation.

<Toggle title="Why these three? Are they actually related?">
  They are — and the intersections are the point. Generative models are becoming
  infrastructure, so questions like "how do you serve a diffusion model cheaply" or "how do
  you give an agent a reliable image tool" are Latents, Logs, and Loops questions at the same
  time. My bet: the people who understand both the mechanisms and the systems will be standing
  in the most interesting spot.
</Toggle>

## How to read this garden

Browse by track or by series, not by date. Timelines mean little in a garden — a seed planted in June might turn evergreen in December. If you spot an error, find me on [GitHub](https://github.com/maxx1031); pointing out mistakes is the kindest thing you can do for a gardener.
